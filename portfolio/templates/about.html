How I build

Greed Island started as a paper RPG with friends, using a calculator for randomness — design a build, fight, lose, redesign, fight again, refining stat allocations and counters round after round until we could predict the outcome before even playing it out. I didn't have the vocabulary for it at the time, but that's a fitness loop — propose, test, lose, adjust. Reinforcement learning in miniature, built with nothing but paper and persistence.

That pattern shows up everywhere I've built things: SkynetGrid went through 34 iterations connecting machines into a working grid, not because anyone assigned it, but because the architecture problem kept pulling me back in — I was reaching for distributed systems before I had the formal vocabulary for what I was reaching for.

Ideas carry over between projects more often than they start fresh. A weighted random-selection system I built for a text-based mining game — call it a rarity pool — turned out to be the same shape of problem as probabilistic decision-making for autonomous agents, so it became the seed for how Greed Island's characters choose what to do next. Likewise, an expression parser written to power a calculator grew into a full tokenizer → parser → symbol-table → evaluator pipeline — a small compiler — once I wanted to know what was actually happening underneath "the calculator just works."

Projects on this site are labelled by how they were actually written — Self-coded for the ones built from scratch with no AI assistance, Built with Claude where Claude was a pair-programmer, and Collaborative for team projects. The architecture and the debugging were mine either way; the label just says who did the typing.
